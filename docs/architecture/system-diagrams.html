Realistic Operating Workflow

MerHouse coordinates work between a merchant, a warehouse provider, and platform reviewers. The happy path is deliberately operational: relationship, inbound stock, demand, allocation, warehouse execution, evidence, alerts, and service review.

1. OnboardPlatform reviews access requests, creates tenants and users, and keeps support and auditor users in review-safe roles.
2. ConnectMerchant asks for provider service. Warehouse activates the relationship or platform governs exceptions.
3. StockMerchant creates product records and sends inbound stock. Warehouse approves, receives, rejects, or records damaged quantities.
4. FulfillMerchant creates order demand. Stock is allocated to an authorized warehouse, then picked, packed, shipped, or marked exceptional.
5. ReviewBoth parties inspect timelines, notifications, service records, and accountability evidence before closing the work.

Story: A New Merchant Needs To Go Live

ActorOwner/admin reviews a public access request while support can watch the queue and auditor can inspect the decision later.
TensionThe requester needs an account, but duplicate emails, missing organization details, and accidental seed-style credentials must not slip into a public-facing setup.
WorkPlatform approves or rejects the request, converts approved work into tenant and user records, and leaves the approval trail separate from the conversion actor.
DoneThe merchant logs in to an empty but guided workspace, account-ready delivery history exists, and governance evidence is readable without exposing credentials.

Story: A Merchant Sends The First Pallet

ActorMerchant creates product records, picks an active warehouse relationship, and sends inbound stock to a provider warehouse.
TensionReal receiving is messy: quantities can be short, damaged, rejected, or partially received, and none of that should silently become clean available stock.
WorkWarehouse approves the inbound request, records received and damaged quantities, and the system writes inventory movement evidence.
DoneOnly received quantity becomes available warehouse inventory; the merchant sees the receiving evidence, not just a green success state.

Story: Demand Becomes Warehouse Work

ActorMerchant creates a single order or imports many rows; warehouse operators receive allocated work tied to authorized stock.
TensionStock may be insufficient, copied order quantities may be invalid, and warehouse execution can hit exceptions after picking starts.
WorkThe system allocates what it can, preserves backorder evidence, then warehouse records pick, pack, shipment, package, and exception details.
DoneMerchant and warehouse share the same operational timeline: what shipped, what failed, what returned, and which exception still needs review.

Story: Partners Need A Service Record

ActorMerchant and warehouse agree on operating terms once the relationship is active; platform can inspect the record across tenants.
TensionThe product must capture accountability without pretending to be invoicing, payment collection, legal casework, or attachment storage.
WorkAgreement terms, rate cards, SLA clocks, statements, disputes, claims, and review requests stay linked to the relationship and source records.
DoneBoth parties can review note-and-record evidence, resolve issues, and keep support/auditor users in read-only evidence mode.

Story: Something Needs Attention

ActorAny role may receive local alerts, but each delivery is scoped to the recipient and tenant that actually owns the next action.
TensionUnread work can be buried under history, provider email might be disabled, and background handoff failures still need a review surface.
WorkUnread local deliveries become the action inbox; outbox records preserve retry/dead-letter evidence; assistant drafts stay non-mutating.
DoneThe right person sees the next action, read history remains available, and external-provider claims wait for separate V17 proof.

Story: The Same Product Ships To Web And Android

ActorOperator deploys the React web app, deploys the Spring backend, and builds a signed Android APK against the deployed backend URL.
TensionScripts can pass while the real browser or installed app feels broken, slow, or misconfigured.
WorkLive browser review leads, supporting proof records follow, and Android wraps the same frontend shell instead of creating a second product.
DoneV17 proof shows deployed web and visible installed Android behavior before release evidence is attached to a cutover package.

Workflow Detail: What Actually Happens

Access And Account Lifecycle

Public access requests become reviewed onboarding work. Approval can create tenant and user records, while account-ready and recovery events create local delivery history.

  • empty accounts see guided first-run states
  • owner/admin controls role, tenant, enabled state, and conversion
  • each signed-in user can inspect account context and change their password with current-password verification
  • reset and account-ready delivery are local by default; SMTP attempts are V17 proof work when enabled

Inbound Stock To Available Inventory

The merchant owns goods and requests a provider warehouse to receive them. Only received quantity becomes available warehouse inventory.

  • draft and submitted requests keep merchant intent visible
  • warehouse approval starts receiving work
  • received, damaged, short, rejected, and cancelled states remain evidence
  • inventory audit logs capture quantity changes and actor context

Order Fulfillment

Orders allocate against authorized stock for an active relationship. Warehouse execution creates shipment, package, event, and exception evidence.

  • partial allocation creates allocation items and possible backorder items
  • warehouse operators progress allocations through pick, pack, and ship work
  • shipments can be in transit, delivered, failed, or returned as delivery-state evidence
  • exceptions stay linked to the allocation, shipment, merchant, and provider parties

Service Accountability

Service records describe operating terms and evidence, not billing or legal settlement. They keep the merchant/provider relationship reviewable.

  • agreements, rate cards, and SLA policies attach to active relationships
  • statements and statement lines summarize local service units
  • claims, disputes, and reviews preserve note-and-record evidence
  • support and auditor roles review the evidence without service mutation controls

Notifications, Outbox, Assistant

Operational handoffs create recipient-scoped local alerts. Reliability records make asynchronous work and review assistance visible without pretending to be external automation.

  • notification preferences are scoped by user, topic, and channel
  • unread local deliveries are an action inbox; mixed delivery records remain history
  • outbox events and carrier dispatch records capture local handoff attempts
  • assistant interactions remain read-plus-draft and non-mutating

Web, Android, And Release Proof

The React product shell is shared by desktop web, narrow web, and Android packaging. The V17 release lane deploys the same product rather than a second mobile implementation.

  • web runtime is deployed through Vercel
  • backend runtime is packaged for a Hugging Face Docker Space
  • data is expected to live in Neon PostgreSQL for the managed lane
  • signed Android APK delivery is through GitHub Releases after live proof

State Machines

These are the states operators actually see and reason about. They omit code names that do not change the user workflow.

Relationship

Requested Active Suspended Ended

Warehouse can activate requested work for its provider tenant. Platform governance owns suspend, reactivate, and end actions.

Inbound Stock

Draft Submitted Approved Receiving Received or Rejected

Cancelled can branch from merchant-owned work before completion; damaged and short quantities remain evidence.

Fulfillment

Demand Created Allocated or Backordered Picking Packed Shipped Delivered, Failed, Returned, or Exceptional

Backorder evidence does not pretend an allocation exists. Exceptions stay reviewable beside shipment evidence.

Service Agreement

Draft Terms Proposed Active Superseded

Review requests stay locked until an agreement is active; statements remain service-unit evidence, not invoices.

Notification Delivery

Prepared Local Recorded or Skipped Unread Action or History Read Provider Recorded When Enabled

The action inbox is recipient-scoped. Provider delivery is evidence only after V17 SMTP proof.

Runtime And Release Shape

Local Certified Shape

Local proof keeps the product coherent while V17 deployed evidence gates production claims.

  • Docker Compose runs PostgreSQL, Spring backend, and nginx-served frontend for local review
  • browser tours and installed Android tours verify the same stakeholder workflows
  • proof reports support live review but do not replace it
  • shared mobile shell proof rejects duplicate product behavior inside the Android wrapper

Selected V17 Managed Lane

The current no-card deployed lane keeps the first release simple and stable without requiring a real domain.

  • Neon PostgreSQL for managed data
  • Hugging Face Docker Space for the Spring backend
  • Vercel for the React frontend
  • GitHub Releases for signed internal APK distribution
  • SMTP proof only after provider credentials are externalized and verified
